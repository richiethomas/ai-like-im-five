Training a CNN from scratch is expensive. You need:
- Hundreds of thousands of images
- Weeks of GPU compute
- Careful hyperparameter tuning

If you're building a classifier for a niche task (detecting disease in medical scans), you might not have that data.

**Transfer learning** solves this. Reuse a network trained on a large, public dataset (like ImageNet, which has millions of images and thousands of categories) as a starting point. Adapt it to your task.

## How It Works

Pre-trained networks have learned general visual features: edges, textures, shapes, objects. These features are useful for almost any vision task.

Take a pre-trained network. Remove the final classification layer (which predicts ImageNet categories). Replace it with a new classification layer for your task (e.g., "disease or healthy").

Now you have two choices:

**Option 1: Fine-tuning**

Train the entire network (including pre-trained layers) on your small dataset, but with a low learning rate. The pre-trained weights are a good starting point; small adjustments adapt them to your task.

This works well when you have a fair amount of data: enough examples that adjusting all the weights improves the fit instead of just overfitting.

**Option 2: Feature extraction**

Freeze the pre-trained layers (don't update their weights). Only train the new final layer.

This is faster and needs less data. You're treating the pre-trained network as a feature extractor: it converts your images to high-level features, and you just train a simple classifier (often a single new layer) on top.

This works well when your data is scarce, too little to safely retrain the whole network without overfitting. It relies on the borrowed features being relevant, so it works best when your task resembles the everyday objects ImageNet was trained on; the more your images differ from those, the less the frozen features help.

## The Hiring Analogy

Imagine hiring someone with years of general experience in the field versus a total beginner. The experienced hire still has to learn your specific job, but they pick it up far faster because they already have the foundation. Transfer learning is similar.

A network trained on ImageNet already understands vision in general. Adapting it to your specific task is much faster than teaching it to see from zero.

## Impact

Transfer learning is what puts state-of-the-art CNNs within reach of people without giant datasets or compute budgets. Instead of the hundreds of thousands of images and weeks of GPU time that training from scratch demands, you can build a competitive system with modest data and a pre-trained network.
